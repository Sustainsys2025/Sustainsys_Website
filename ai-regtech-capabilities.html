The Problem

Why Regulatory Reporting Is Breaking Banks

Global banks face an unprecedented convergence of reporting demands. Multiple regimes, evolving schemas, conflicting data definitions, and accelerating regulatory timelines have pushed manual compliance processes beyond their limits.

Regulatory Fragmentation

A single derivatives desk may need to report under EU EMIR, UK EMIR, CFTC Parts 43/45, MiFIR RTS 22, and MAS regulations simultaneously — each with different field definitions, validation rules, XML schemas, and submission formats. EU EMIR alone mandates 203 reportable fields; UK EMIR requires 204. Reconciling overlapping but non-identical requirements across regimes consumes thousands of analyst hours annually.

Constant Regulatory Change

EMIR Refit went live April 2024. UK EMIR Refit followed in September 2024. CFTC rewrote its tech specifications in January 2024 with 49 further fields proposed. MiFIR RTS 22 revisions are expected in 2026. Basel III.1 implementation began January 2025. CSRD mandates sustainability reporting in waves through 2028. Tracking these changes manually across regulatory portals is operationally unsustainable.

Data Quality and Lineage Pressure

BCBS 239 principles on risk data aggregation demand that banks demonstrate complete data lineage from source system to regulatory report. Yet most institutions still operate with inconsistent identifiers (LEI, UTI, UPI), fragmented source systems, and manual reconciliation processes that introduce latency and error. Regulators are increasingly auditing data quality at the field level.

Manual Interpretation of Regulations

Regulatory technical standards (RTS/ITS), validation tables, XML/XSD schemas, reporting instructions, and regulator Q&A documents can run to thousands of pages per regime. ESMA’s EMIR validation table alone contains 200+ rules across multiple worksheets. Interpreting these documents, identifying changes between versions, and translating requirements into system specifications is a bottleneck in every regulatory programme.

Our Approach

The SustainsysAI Approach

Rather than replacing existing reporting infrastructure, SustainsysAI provides an intelligent regulatory knowledge layer that sits alongside bank systems — parsing regulatory source material, building structured knowledge representations, and delivering precise, citation-backed regulatory insights to reporting teams.

🧠

Frontier LLM Models

The platform leverages frontier language models to interpret regulatory documentation, extract structured requirements from unstructured text, and generate implementation guidance grounded in source material. Multi-model architecture routes queries to specialised models based on task type.

🔍

RAG + CAG Pipeline

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and Context-Augmented Generation (CAG) ensure that every response is grounded in actual regulatory source documents. Vector embeddings of regulatory text enable semantic search across regimes, while context augmentation provides the model with precise, relevant passages.

🌐

Regulatory Knowledge Graph

Structured relationships between regimes, fields, validation rules, ISO standards, and reporting entities are maintained in a purpose-built knowledge graph. This enables cross-regime comparison, impact propagation analysis, and automated dependency mapping that would be impossible with flat document stores.

Document Chunking & Embedding

Regulatory documents are chunked using structure-aware splitting that respects section boundaries, table structures, and article numbering. Each chunk is embedded into a vector space using domain-tuned models, enabling semantic retrieval that understands regulatory terminology beyond keyword matching.

Multi-Model Routing

The architecture employs a multi-model strategy: specialised models handle schema parsing, validation rule extraction, and field comparison, while frontier models handle complex reasoning tasks such as cross-regime gap analysis and regulatory change impact assessment. Routing logic selects the appropriate model based on query classification.

Field Intelligence

Field-Level Impact Intelligence

The platform operates at the granularity of individual regulatory fields — the atomic unit of regulatory reporting. Every field is mapped, compared, validated, and scored across regimes to provide actionable implementation intelligence.

🗂

Canonical Field Mapping

58 canonical fields are maintained as a cross-regime harmonised data model. Each canonical field maps to its regime-specific variants, capturing differences in naming, data type, format, and validation requirements. This enables a single source system to serve multiple regulatory outputs.

🔄

Cross-Regime Comparison

For any given field (e.g., Unique Transaction Identifier), the platform compares the definition, validation logic, mandatoriness (M/C/O), data type, and ISO standard alignment across all active regimes. This identifies convergence points and regime-specific deviations that affect implementation.

📊

Regulatory Impact Scoring

When a regulatory change is detected — a new field, modified validation rule, or schema update — the platform automatically scores the impact based on the number of affected regimes, downstream system dependencies, and implementation complexity. This feeds directly into the implementation backlog.

Enterprise Integration

Integration with Bank AI Ecosystems

SustainsysAI is designed to operate within existing bank technology governance frameworks — integrating with internal LLM platforms, model risk management processes, and data governance structures rather than bypassing them.

🔒

Model Risk Management

All AI-generated regulatory insights include full provenance: the source documents consulted, the retrieval scores, and the generation parameters. This supports SR 11-7 model risk management requirements and internal validation processes. Models can be swapped or updated without changing the knowledge layer.

🔗

Internal LLM Integration

Banks running internal LLM platforms (Azure OpenAI, AWS Bedrock, on-premise deployments) can route SustainsysAI queries through their own infrastructure. The regulatory knowledge graph and RAG pipeline are decoupled from the model layer, enabling bring-your-own-model deployments.

📋

Governance & Audit

Every interaction is logged with complete audit trails. Regulatory citations link directly to official source documents. The platform supports role-based access control, data residency requirements, and integration with bank SIEM systems for security monitoring.

Explainability

Every regulatory answer includes a citation chain: the question asked, the documents retrieved, the passages used for generation, and the confidence score. Bank compliance teams can verify any AI-generated guidance against the underlying regulatory source.

Supervisory Alignment

The architecture is designed to align with emerging supervisory expectations for AI in financial services — including ECB guidance on AI risk, PRA expectations on model governance, and MAS principles on responsible AI use in regulated activities.

The Case for Action

Why Banks Need AI-Driven Regulatory Intelligence Now

The regulatory landscape is not simplifying. The volume of reporting requirements, the pace of regulatory change, and the supervisory expectation for data quality are all accelerating. Manual approaches have reached their operational limit.

Regime Convergence Creates Opportunity

The global shift to ISO 20022 XML, standardised identifiers (LEI, UTI, UPI), and harmonised CDE definitions means that for the first time, a single canonical data model can serve multiple regimes. But exploiting this convergence requires the ability to map fields across regimes at scale — precisely the capability that AI-driven knowledge graphs provide.

Regulatory Velocity Exceeds Manual Capacity

Between 2024 and 2028, banks face simultaneous implementation deadlines for EMIR Refit, CFTC rewrites, Basel III.1, CSRD, DORA, MiFIR revisions, APAC rewrites, and the EU AML package. Any institution attempting to track these changes manually across dozens of regulatory portals is accepting material operational risk.

Data Quality Is Now a Regulatory Priority

Regulators are increasingly scrutinising data quality at the field level. BCBS 239 compliance, DORA operational resilience requirements, and enhanced transaction reporting validation all demand that banks can demonstrate end-to-end data lineage from source system to regulatory submission. AI-driven field mapping provides this traceability.

Competitive Differentiation Through Intelligence

Banks that build regulatory intelligence capabilities now — understanding not just what to report, but how regulatory changes propagate through their systems — will have a structural advantage in implementation speed, compliance quality, and operational cost. Regulatory reporting is no longer a cost centre to minimise; it is an operational capability to optimise.
